Two staking operators publish their monthly report on the same day. One says it runs 64 validators.
The other says it runs one. They are holding the same amount of capital.

Both statements are true, and neither is a rounding error. Since EIP-7251 shipped in
the Pectra upgrade, the mainnet preset sets `MIN_ACTIVATION_BALANCE` to 32 ETH and
`MAX_EFFECTIVE_BALANCE_ELECTRA` to 2048 ETH, so a single validator record can hold anything from 32
up to 2048 ETH of effective balance.<Cite src="consensus-specs-mainnet-presets" /><Cite src="eip-7251" />
Two thousand and forty-eight ETH is 64 seats at the old unit of account, or one seat at the new one.

That is the first thing to unlearn, and it is not a trivia correction. Almost every published
statement about Ethereum's validator set — how decentralised it is, how much it would cost to attack,
what a client bug would take down — was written when validator count and stake were the same number
scaled by 32. They are not any more. **Reason in ETH.**

This lesson is about what the deposit actually buys, what the record actually contains, and why the
same object is simultaneously the anti-Sybil mechanism and the bond.

## The record has eight fields and none of them is you

Strip away the dashboards and a validator is a `Validator` container in the beacon state, with
exactly these fields: a BLS public key, withdrawal credentials, an effective balance, a slashed flag,
and four epoch numbers — activation eligibility, activation, exit, and
withdrawable.<Cite src="consensus-specs-phase0-beacon-chain" />

Read that list for what is missing. There is no operator identity, no IP address, no machine, no
company, no jurisdiction. The protocol does not know that your 64 records are one entity, and it does
not know that someone else's one record is a pooled product with ten thousand depositors behind it.
It knows a key and a balance.

This is [Sybil resistance](/c/sybil-resistance) done the only way an open system can do it. The
protocol cannot verify that two participants are different people, so it stops trying and prices
influence in the one thing that cannot be forged by creating more identities: capital that is
verifiably locked and destructible. Splitting your stake across more keys buys you nothing, because
weight is denominated in balance, not in records.

## Two keys, two threat models

A seat needs two credentials, and conflating them is how people lose money.

The **signing key** is the BLS key in the `pubkey` field. It lives in the validator client, online,
and signs blocks and attestations several times an hour. It is hot by necessity.

The **withdrawal credentials** are a separate 32-byte field that says where the money goes when it
eventually leaves. Since Electra a third prefix exists alongside the earlier two:
`COMPOUNDING_WITHDRAWAL_PREFIX`, the byte `0x02`, marks a validator whose maximum effective balance is
`MAX_EFFECTIVE_BALANCE_ELECTRA` rather than
`MIN_ACTIVATION_BALANCE`.<Cite src="consensus-specs-electra-beacon-chain" /><Cite src="eip-7251" />
The 2048 ETH ceiling is not automatic — the spec's `get_max_effective_balance` returns the higher
figure only for validators carrying that credential prefix.<Cite src="consensus-specs-electra-beacon-chain" />

The asymmetry to hold on to: **the signing key can get your stake slashed, and cannot move it. The
withdrawal credentials can move it, and cannot get it slashed.** A staking service that holds your
signing key holds your risk; one that holds your withdrawal credentials holds your money. Those are
different questions and they get asked as one.

## Effective balance is not your balance

The state carries two balances per validator and they deliberately disagree.

Your *actual* balance moves every epoch, up by rewards and down by penalties. Your *effective*
balance — the number that determines your voting weight, your rewards, and every penalty computed
against you — is a coarse, sticky quantisation of it. It moves in whole units of
`EFFECTIVE_BALANCE_INCREMENT`, which the mainnet preset sets to 1 ETH, and only when the actual
balance drifts past a hysteresis threshold set by `HYSTERESIS_QUOTIENT` of 4 with downward and upward
multipliers of 1 and 5.<Cite src="consensus-specs-mainnet-presets" /><Cite src="consensus-specs-electra-beacon-chain" />

Concretely: a validator's effective balance falls only when the actual balance drops a quarter of an
ETH below it, and rises only when the actual balance climbs one and a quarter ETH above
it.<Cite src="consensus-specs-electra-beacon-chain" /> The dead band is the point. Without it, every
validator's weight would change every epoch, and the state would be a churning field of
recomputations for no consensus benefit.

The practical consequence is that a validator sitting exactly at the minimum is one bad week away
from an effective balance below 32 ETH, and there is a floor underneath. `process_registry_updates` initiates an exit
for any active validator whose effective balance has fallen to `EJECTION_BALANCE` or below, and the
mainnet config sets that at 16
ETH.<Cite src="consensus-specs-phase0-beacon-chain" /><Cite src="consensus-specs-config-mainnet" />
Nobody asks your permission.

## Getting in is a queue, and the queue is denominated in ETH

You do not join by announcing yourself. You send a deposit to the staking contract on the execution
layer, and since EIP-6110 those deposits are supplied to the consensus layer as in-protocol requests
in the block itself rather than being observed through a delayed voting
mechanism.<Cite src="eip-6110" />

Then you wait, and what you wait for changed shape in Electra. The activation queue used to be
counted in validators per epoch. It is now counted in **balance** per epoch: `get_balance_churn_limit`
returns the larger of `MIN_PER_EPOCH_CHURN_LIMIT_ELECTRA` — 128 ETH — and the total active balance
divided by `CHURN_LIMIT_QUOTIENT`, which is 65,536; activations and exits are then capped at
`MAX_PER_EPOCH_ACTIVATION_EXIT_CHURN_LIMIT`, 256
ETH.<Cite src="consensus-specs-electra-beacon-chain" /><Cite src="consensus-specs-config-mainnet" />

Do the arithmetic on the cap and it tells you something: 256 × 65,536 is 16,777,216, so once total
active balance exceeds roughly 16.8M ETH the cap binds and the queue moves at a flat 256 ETH per
epoch regardless of how large the network gets.<Cite src="consensus-specs-config-mainnet" /> Mainnet
passed that level some time ago. Published figures for total staked ETH disagree with one another,
so take a live reading rather than any number printed in a
lesson.<Cite src="ethereum-org-staking" /><Cite src="theblock-staking-share" />

Once activated, there is one more delay before your duties are real: the honest-validator
specification says a newly activated validator is added to the shuffling and begins validating after
an additional `MAX_SEED_LOOKAHEAD` epochs, which the mainnet preset sets to
4.<Cite src="consensus-specs-validator" /><Cite src="consensus-specs-mainnet-presets" /> The same
text notes that the wait before activation is itself variable, because it depends on finality, total
active balance and the length of the queue ahead of
you.<Cite src="consensus-specs-validator" /><Cite src="mixbytes-validator-lifecycle" />

## Getting out is a longer queue, plus a delay you cannot buy your way past

Exit is the mirror image with two extra frictions.

First, you cannot leave immediately after arriving. `process_voluntary_exit` asserts that the current
epoch is at least the validator's activation epoch plus `SHARD_COMMITTEE_PERIOD`, which the mainnet
config sets to 256 epochs.<Cite src="consensus-specs-electra-beacon-chain" /><Cite src="consensus-specs-config-mainnet" />

Second, after your exit epoch arrives, the funds are still not movable. `initiate_validator_exit`
sets the withdrawable epoch to the exit epoch plus `MIN_VALIDATOR_WITHDRAWABILITY_DELAY`, another 256
epochs.<Cite src="consensus-specs-electra-beacon-chain" /><Cite src="consensus-specs-config-mainnet" />

Since EIP-7002 the exit can also be triggered from the execution layer, by the address in the
withdrawal credentials, without the signing key participating at
all.<Cite src="eip-7002" /> That closes a real operational hole: before it, a staker whose operator
had disappeared with the signing key had no way to get their capital out.

<Aside kind="note">
Notice the shape of all four delays — activation churn, seed lookahead, the minimum active period,
the withdrawability delay. None of them is about processing time. They exist so that the validator
set changes slowly relative to the time it takes to detect and punish misbehaviour. An attacker who
could enter, act and leave inside a few epochs would face a penalty mechanism with nothing left to
penalise.
</Aside>

## What the seat actually buys

Two duties, on different clocks.

**Attesting** happens every epoch, for every active validator, without exception. This is the bulk of
the work and the bulk of the reward.<Cite src="ethereum-docs-pos" />

**Proposing** happens when you are selected, which is a lottery weighted by effective balance, and for
a validator at the minimum balance it is rare enough to be treated as a windfall rather than a
schedule.<Cite src="ethereum-docs-pos" />

Both are duties in the strict sense: failing to perform them costs you. The seat is not an
entitlement to earn, it is an obligation with a yield attached, and the next lesson is about how
tightly that obligation is scheduled.

<Check type="explain" concept="validator">
An operator has 2048 ETH to stake and asks whether to run it as one validator with compounding
credentials or as 64 validators at 32 ETH each. Name one thing that genuinely differs between the two
and one thing that does not — and say what the count actually changes.

<Answer>
**What does not differ: consensus weight, rewards, and the size of a slashing charge.** All three are
computed from effective balance, and 64 × 32 is 2048 either way. The protocol has no notion of
"an operator" to treat differently.<Cite src="eip-7251" />

**What does differ: operational surface and granularity.** Sixty-four records means 64 keys to manage,
64 sets of duties to track, and 64 opportunities to misconfigure something — but it also means the
operator can exit or move a 32 ETH slice without touching the rest, and the exit queue is entered per
record. One record with compounding credentials means a single key, automatic compounding above 32
ETH up to the 2048 ETH ceiling, and an all-or-nothing exit.<Cite src="consensus-specs-electra-beacon-chain" />

**What the count changes for you as a reader is measurement.** If you are assessing how concentrated
staking is, or what a bug in one client would cost, counting validators now measures nothing in
particular. The 64-record operator and the 1-record operator are the same amount of stake and the
same amount of risk. Only the ETH figure carries meaning, which is why this module's measurement
practice asks for total active balance rather than a validator count.
</Answer>
</Check>

## Where this pays off

The deposit being simultaneously a Sybil defence and a bond is the hinge for everything that follows.
Because it is a Sybil defence, the protocol can hand out duties by weight without knowing who anyone
is — which is what makes the committee sampling two lessons from now safe. Because it is a bond, the
protocol has something to take when it catches a contradiction, which is what makes the three
slashable offences worth defining at all.

And the ETH-not-count discipline pays off immediately in the finality module, where the cost of
reversing the chain is a fraction of total stake. That fraction has a meaning. A fraction of the
validator count no longer does.
